The source sections for this task are EXPERIMENTS.md §8.1, §9.2, §10.2, §11, §12.1, §13.1 and §§14–18.

Implement wavelength-by-wavelength equivalent-time reconstruction, not a nanosecond full MIRcat scan. Support RT-HRP-G / ARC-RT-HRP-NS, RT-Mb-G / ARC-RT-MB-NS, 77K-HRP-G-F / ARC-77-HRP-NS, and the nanosecond branch of 77K-Mb-G-F / ARC-77-MB-NSUS. These labels are profiles and traceability identities, not assumptions that a resolved lifetime is achievable.

At each selected measured wavenumber, reach accepted tuning/readiness and settling, complete the pump–probe delay series with equivalent sample resets and averaging, then move to the next wavenumber. Plan multiple negative delays beyond measured IRF support, dense response/early recovery coverage, justified later/bridge delays, applicable pump-blocked/off-band controls and randomized or counterbalanced order. Store requested, quantized, observed electrical and calibrated optical delays separately. Use nanosecond-appropriate input/display units without claiming precision finer than the installed timing/IRF evidence.

The critical backend deliverable is a physically valid, installed-wiring-compatible equivalent-time measurement kernel. Show how changing optical pump-to-probe delay changes the spectral observable retained by HF2LI, with justified probe pulse selection, synchronization, integration aperture, filter history and reset. Merely programming ns delays while recording a continuous slow HF2LI trace is insufficient. Do not equate a tiny T660 step, nominal MIRcat pulse width or HF2LI timestamp with system resolution, and do not replace HF2LI spectra with PicoScope data. If the installed routes/readout cannot implement the proposed kernel, finish the software and simulator, document the exact missing capability/qualification and make connected Start explain that specific readiness condition rather than pretending that generic averaging solves it.

Build IRF/noise-aware forward simulation and event-budget planning before accepting a confirmatory schedule. RT HRP should support both sample-fitted CO populations; RT MbCO is A1-first and extends to A0/A3 only where quantified. Cryogenic repetitions require demonstrated equivalent-state reset/fresh-position identity; ordinary delay stepping must never accumulate unacknowledged photoproduct. Do not fix the historical ~180 ns, ~4% MbCO values or a sub-100-ns HRP response.

Retain every event and its wavelength, delay, condition, technical repetition, pump evidence and acquisition kernel; reconstruct native supported Delta A(nu,t), local spectra, band/point kinetics, coverage and uncertainty. Include convolved fits, identifiability tests and a prompt/unresolved-bound outcome when lifetime information is absent. The UI should show the delay schedule, measurement kernel/resolution, projected event and reset burden, spectral/kinetic maps and linked slices. Tests must demonstrate unbiased recovery when resolvable and honest non-identifiability when jitter, aperture, filtering, noise or reset failure makes the target unresolvable.
